# Breaking the stigma without breaking the law

What Hims, Ro and Alto actually do, and what to take.

Hims puts Hair, Sex, Skin, Weight and Mental health in the main navigation at equal weight. The move is not the bold type; it is that nothing is hidden behind a "wellness" euphemism. Take that: sexual health, mind and sleep, and periods sit in the same eight-tile grid as diabetes, same icon well, same one-line, same hover. The stigma lives in any difference of treatment, so there is none.

Ro opens with a question ("What can we help with?"), names its clinicians, and prints prices in a plain table. Take the named clinician (the pharmacist's name and registration number on the seal and in the footer is your version) and the plain price (printed MRP, no theatre).

Alto is pharmacist-forward and unhurried: the pharmacist is the hero of the brand, not the app. Take that framing for the verification step: it's care, not policing. The copy says "checks", never "approves" or "screens".

Where India differs. The Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act bans advertising drugs for a list of conditions that includes diabetes and sexual disorders, and Schedule H drugs can't be advertised. So the concern tiles and hubs describe the condition and the service, never a drug as a treatment; the drug names live on the product and molecule pages that people reach by searching. Editorial is educational with reviewer bylines and sources, not promotional. No discounts anywhere solves most of the rest.

What makes it feel like a billion-dollar product rather than a template is restraint you can see: one typeface doing the work, one accent colour, one shadow in the whole site (under the blister strip), one motion moment per page, medicine shown as typography, and copy that says the specific true thing. Every time Cursor reaches for a stats row, a gradient or a carousel, it is reaching for the opposite.

The spot illustrations are where warmth comes from, and they are also where AI-generated SVG is weakest. If a drawing comes out stiff, replace it with the striped PhotoPlaceholder and a precise caption, and get the real thing shot later: a steel tumbler, morning chai, a tablet on a steel plate, a doorstep handover. Those four photographs, done properly, will do more for the brand than any illustration system.
