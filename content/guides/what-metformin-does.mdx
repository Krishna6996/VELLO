If you have type 2 diabetes, there is a good chance the first tablet your doctor wrote was metformin. It has been in use since the 1950s, it is on the WHO list of essential medicines, and in India it sits behind dozens of brand names: Glycomet, Carbophage, Gluformin, Glyciphage and the plain Jan Aushadhi strip. This guide is about the molecule itself. Whatever the box says, the medicine inside is the same.

## What it does

Type 2 diabetes is not a shortage of insulin so much as a body that has stopped listening to it. Sugar stays in the blood instead of moving into the muscles and liver where it is used or stored, and the liver keeps releasing more sugar even when there is plenty already.

Metformin works on that second problem most of all. It tells the liver to release less sugar, and it helps muscle respond to the insulin the body is already making. It does not push the pancreas to make more insulin. That single fact explains most of what is good about it: taken on its own, metformin very rarely causes low blood sugar, and it does not tend to cause weight gain. The ADA and ICMR guidelines both list it as the usual first medicine for type 2 diabetes for those reasons.

The effect is modest and steady. Most people see their HbA1c, the three-month average of blood sugar, come down by roughly one to one and a half percentage points. It is not dramatic on any one day. It is a small correction applied every day for years, and the long UKPDS trial showed that this is what protects the eyes, kidneys and heart.

## What the first weeks feel like

The most common complaint is the stomach. Loose motions, nausea, wind, a metallic taste, less appetite. For most people this settles within two to three weeks as the gut adjusts. Three things help: take it with or straight after a meal, never on an empty stomach; start at the dose your doctor wrote rather than a higher one; and if the plain tablet troubles you, ask whether a sustained-release version, the kind marked SR, would suit. SR tablets release the medicine slowly over the day and are gentler on the stomach. Swallow them whole. Crushing an SR tablet defeats the coating.

## What it doesn't do

It does not cure diabetes. Stopping it means the liver goes back to what it was doing before.

It does not replace the rest of the plan. Food, walking and sleep still move the sugar more than any tablet does, and metformin works better alongside them, not instead of them.

It does not work for everyone on its own for ever. Type 2 diabetes tends to progress, and many people need a second medicine after some years. This is not a failure of the tablet or of the patient. It is the nature of the condition.

It does not treat type 1 diabetes, where the pancreas has stopped making insulin altogether. People with type 1 need insulin.

## Things your doctor will watch

Kidneys. Metformin leaves the body through the kidneys, so your doctor will check a blood test called eGFR before starting and every year or so after. If kidney function falls, the dose is reduced or the medicine is stopped. This is routine, not alarming.

Vitamin B12. Long use can lower B12 in some people. A blood test every few years catches it, and a supplement fixes it.

Days when you are very unwell. If you are vomiting, badly dehydrated, or about to have a scan with contrast dye or an operation, your doctor may ask you to pause metformin for a day or two. Ask before a procedure rather than after.

## Signs to act on

Stop the tablet and call your doctor the same day if you have severe vomiting, stomach pain with fast breathing, unusual drowsiness or muscle pain that does not make sense. These are rare, but they are the signs of a serious build-up of acid in the blood that metformin can, very occasionally, cause when the kidneys are struggling.

## About the brands

Every brand of metformin at the same strength and in the same form, tablet or SR, contains the same molecule and has to meet the same standard to be sold in India. The differences are the coating, the shape, the name on the strip and the printed price. The Jan Aushadhi strip is the same medicine at the government's price. Your doctor may have a reason to prefer one brand, and it is fair to ask them. It is also fair to choose the one you can afford. What matters is that you take the same strength, in the same form, at the same times, every day.
